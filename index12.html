Curatorial Statement
The dream of flying is as old as humanity. Hot air balloons were the first objects to make humans fly over longer distances. We can imagine their invention to be born out of both: an innocent human curiosity, and the strategic interest to chart and explore territory. In any case, the ability to fly changed the way humans have seen the earth and interacted with it ever since.
There is something strange and poetic about turning a balloon into an exhibition space. Formerly filled with hot air and flying in the sky this yellow giant now provides space for bodies to explore its inside on the ground. The balloon can be seen as an invitation to change the perspective from the bird’s eye view of a removed observer to an involved point of view from within. This space invites not to look for something but to be with others, in space, time, sound and scent.
When experimenting with the balloon, we faced a material so expansive and unwieldy that the six of us could barely manage to move or control. This situation reminded us what it is like to relate to bigger entities beyond individual power such as a state administration, a specific industry or an educational institution. These relations tend to be highly ambiguous due to the gradient of scale and power between its parts. Those entities can attract, assemble, activate and serve, while simultaneously having the tendency to incorporate, accumulate, trap and absorb the ones involved with them. We find ourselves constantly moving between keeping our own agency and handing over control. Here one needs to come up with strategies to navigate, operate and appropriate them. Our strategy in relation to this yellow giant has been collective, distributed, multilayered and experimental.
In the spirit of an open-ended design process, we are inviting other practitioners and visitors to multiply the connections made so far by exploring and appropriating the space in their own ways. In a complimentary gesture YELL, LOW HIGH counteracts a tendency of the Basel Academy of Art & Design: its hyper focus on cognitive and visual knowledge cultures. In this endeavor we think with Bonaventure Son Beijing Ndikung and his concept of “Corpoliteracy'' which considers the body as “a platform, stage, site and medium of learning, a structure or organ that acquires, stores, and disseminates knowledge”*. We aim to create a space that is accommodating, hospitable and reveals itself intuitively based on embodied exploration and without further explanation. It functions as an immersive set: a playground for many.
At CIVIC, YELL, LOW HIGH acts as a public experiment of body power on the presence and the role of bodies in civic action.
Today, the dream of flying keeps evolving towards extensive explorations of outer space. Meanwhile, humanity hasn't figured out yet how to co-exist on the ground of the earth. On this very fragile ground, the balloon continues its journey as something new and weirdly alive. It is calling you to enter and become a part - Get low with it!

Description

In-between worlds is a comisioned artistic intervention at the construction site of Kulturraffinerie K714. Right next to the Rhine a former shell refinery is being transformed into a state-of-the-art concert hall. This transition is accompanied by a process of cultural mediation which looks into the collective memory, the present social and political climate and the future imagination around the place and local community. The project was preceded by an in-depth archival and participatory research which involved voices and imagery from diverse stakeholders. In the form of a Gesamtkunstwerk the piece combined classical music, dance, installation, video and electronic sound art and walked visitors through different stages of the construction site by staging four movements of transition.
